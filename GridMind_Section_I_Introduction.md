# I. INTRODUCTION

Reliable electricity supports Egypt's households, public services, and economic activity. Egypt's continuing expansion of renewable generation makes transmission-network development an important national priority. The European Investment Bank's Egypt Grid Modernization and Expansion project supports new substations and transmission lines to accommodate renewable energy [1]. Similarly, the European Bank for Reconstruction and Development's NWFE Electricity Grid Reinforcement project includes upgrading a Cairo substation and constructing a transmission line to carry renewable generation from the Gulf of Suez region [2]. These initiatives establish a concrete Egyptian motivation for investigating decision-support methods alongside physical infrastructure development. GridMind examines how artificial intelligence (AI) could help engineers interpret operating information and assess potential interventions within this changing energy context.

The technical problem involves several connected questions: whether measurements are sufficiently complete, how demand and renewable production may change, what an abnormal signal represents, how an initiating outage could propagate, and which response could improve the operating state. Research identifies missing measurements as a challenge for state estimation [3], while forecasting studies address variability in demand and renewable generation [4]. Event diagnosis and cascade prediction address different aspects of disturbance interpretation and consequence assessment [5], [6]. These are general power-system challenges; the cited studies do not establish their prevalence in Egypt or demonstrate shortcomings in Egyptian control centers. The project therefore investigates a workflow relevant to Egypt's modernization context while requiring local evidence before claiming operational applicability.

Existing research provides useful foundations for each capability. Li et al. propose a spatio-temporal graph neural network for recovering missing measurements using network relationships and temporal information [3]. Bolusteve et al. compare direct net-load forecasting with an indirect approach that separately predicts demand, wind, and solar generation [4]. Shadi et al. develop a hierarchical framework for detecting, classifying, and locating disturbances from phasor measurements [5]. For cascade assessment, Sevak et al. propose physics-informed graph neural jump ordinary differential equations that model continuous redistribution and discrete failures [6]. In corrective operation, de Jong et al. investigate imitation learning for topology actions and combine learned predictions with additional simulations [7]. The forecasting and cascade references [4], [6] are preprints, and reference [7] is cited in its preprint version; their findings are considered within their reported experimental settings.

These contributions do not automatically establish a validated workflow connecting all five tasks. Their inputs, targets, networks, and evaluation conditions differ, making interface compatibility and downstream usefulness separate research questions. Shadi et al. report that missing phasor measurements can substantially reduce event-localization performance in their smaller test systems [5]. Sevak et al. discuss limitations associated with their cascade simulations and operational applicability [6]. Moreover, the topology-action setting studied by de Jong et al. differs from generator redispatch, so its results cannot directly establish performance for another action space [7]. Taken together, these observations motivate a structured review of available evidence, suitable methods, tool capabilities, and integration requirements. They also motivate evaluating model accuracy alongside the effects of incomplete inputs and the operational outcomes of suggested actions.

GridMind is proposed as a software-based, human-in-the-loop platform combining missing-telemetry reconstruction, demand and renewable forecasting, event diagnosis, cascade-risk prediction, and corrective-action recommendation. Reconstruction would estimate eligible missing readings while retaining their estimated status. Forecasting would provide one-hour-ahead demand, solar, and wind predictions. Diagnosis would distinguish measurement problems from physical abnormalities, with localization evaluated where suitable labels exist. Cascade assessment would estimate consequences conditional on an identified or hypothetical initiating outage. Corrective-action analysis would compare candidate interventions through simulator-based checks before presenting them for engineer review. Shared network analysis would connect these capabilities, including explicitly mapping aggregate forecasts into benchmark operating scenarios. A dashboard would display outputs, provenance, assumptions, and validation outcomes.

The initial system will use public datasets and benchmark or simulated networks. Egypt provides the application motivation; benchmark results will not be presented as validation on Egypt's national grid. Operational use would require representative Egyptian data, appropriate access arrangements, and assessment with local electricity-sector specialists. An existing forecasting proof of concept provides preliminary implementation progress, while the integrated five-module platform remains proposed. The overall aim is to investigate whether this platform can provide useful, traceable decision support under defined experimental conditions. Its objectives are to:

1. Synthesize research across the five capabilities and identify evidence-supported gaps.
2. Develop and evaluate suitable module implementations against task-appropriate baselines.
3. Integrate module outputs with consistent timestamps, units, component identifiers, and provenance.
4. Evaluate individual performance, integrated behavior, and simulator-checked corrective actions while preserving human review.

The review and proposed design are guided by five research questions:

- **RQ1 — State of the art:** What research and solutions exist for AI-based telemetry reconstruction, demand and renewable forecasting, event diagnosis, cascade-risk prediction, and corrective-action recommendation?
- **RQ2 — Methods:** Which technologies, models, datasets, and evaluation techniques support these capabilities, and what performance tradeoffs do they present?
- **RQ3 — Market and tools:** Which academic prototypes, commercial platforms, and open-source tools support these tasks, and how do their capabilities, accessibility, and costs compare?
- **RQ4 — Gaps:** What weaknesses and challenges remain in data quality, generalization, reproducibility, integration, and physical validation?
- **RQ5 — Proposed directions:** What evidence-based improvements could address these gaps through an integrated, transparent platform relevant to Egypt's electricity-modernization context?

Section II describes the review protocol, search strategy, study selection, quality assessment, and evidence synthesis. Section III answers RQ1–RQ5 through comparisons of research findings, methods, tools, gaps, and proposed directions. Section IV interprets these findings and develops the conceptual framework and requirements motivating GridMind. Section V examines threats to validity and limitations affecting the review's conclusions. Section VI details the proposed solution, design rationale, architecture, expected contributions, positioning against existing tools, and evaluation approach. Section VII concludes by summarizing the RQ answers, principal gaps, and research and practical implications.
